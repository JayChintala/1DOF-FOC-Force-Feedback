## Slide 5: Position Based Teleoperation

- The follower motor tracks the position of the leader
- If motor 1 is held down, motor 2 becomes harder to spin as a function of f = k(x1 – x2)
- Not an instant resistance, so we can't tell the difference between squeezing a hard or soft object

Each motor acts like a spring when motors are spun independently

## Slide 6: Velocity based Transmission

- Idea:
- Moving motor 1 creates a velocity, and motor 2 tried to copy it -> this generates a current command for motor 2
- If motor 2 faces resistance, the ESC can sense that resistance as a current
- That resistance current is sent back to motor 1, which is felt by the user
- Reality:
- Can tell between some resistance and a lot of resistance
- The force takes less time to ramp up, but there is still some spring like behavior
- No position control leads to motor drift

## Slide 7: PropHand Influence

- PropHand also used FOC for force sensing
- Position based (would squeeze until reaching target torque)
- Incorporate gearbox at 6:1 to 8:1 ratio for optimal torque transparency  while amplifying torque output from BDLC
- Reintroduce a position term into the controller
- Will help prevent motor drift

## Slide 8: Next Steps

- Swapped to CAN from USB to RPi4 GPIO to reduce latency
- Introduced a position tracking term to eliminate drift
- Testing capstan drives/ gearboxes to increase torque output while maintaining torque transparency

## Slide 9: The Software

*Figure: The Software figure* — diagram_system_notitle.png

Both boards run the ESC_based firmware — 16 kHz FOC current loop, 1 kHz control tick, telemetry every 1 ms. The Pi runs the teleoperation loop at 500 Hz over one CAN bus and consumes every telemetry frame (0 dropped in 60 s). Every run is recorded in TEST_LOG.md — 6,180 lines, 85 dated entries, each with its numbers against a stated pass threshold.

> **Speaker notes:** ESC_based replaced the earlier design in which the Pi computed everything. The coupling law can now run on the ESCs themselves at 1 kHz with the Pi out of the loop, which is what peer-to-peer uses.
>
> Host side: one kernel-filtered CAN socket shared by both motors, RX thread at real-time priority, a per-frame telemetry queue, and logs staged in RAM to avoid SD-card stalls. Velocity and the observers are per-instance classes, with dt taken from the ESC's own microsecond clock rather than a nominal constant.
>
> TEST_LOG.md is the record: every run, its numbers against a stated threshold, PASS or FAIL, the log paths, and the reasoning whenever something failed.

## Slide 10: Finding the External Torque

- How the Pi computes it, every 1 kHz frame (follower motor):
- Divide by Kt and work in amps: τ_env = −( i_q − a/G − friction − gravity ), where G = Kt/J is measured per arm (3.85×10⁶ counts/s² per A for the 3D-printed arm)
- Acceleration a comes from velocity through a 20 Hz filter (no double differentiation), and the current is delayed 3 ms to line up with the velocity estimate
- Friction + gravity are fitted from free motion and subtracted; when the arm is still, τ_env = −i_q, i.e. the current holding the arm still is the contact force

## Slide 11: Teleop Loop

*Figure: Picture 6* — Current control architecture: hand-torque observer feeds follower command; environment-torque observer feeds leader command; a contact latch enables contact springs on both sides.

> **Speaker notes:** Follower Feels: 
> 1. Hand turns leader motor
> 2. The torque the hand is pushing the motor with is estimated based on velocity and current
> 3. The follower motor command is then calculated as the sum of the hand torque, a velocity term to match the leader's velocity, weak position term to also match the follower's position (this prevented drift when the motor was stationary), and a “contact spring” that stops the force from decreasing when the follower is pushing against an object.
>
>
> Leader feels: 
> The motor pushes against an object
> Torque is estimated based on velocity and current.
> Felt force is a constant (Kf) * T_env with a deadband of 20 mA to reduce noise from affecting the feeling. Dampening is added to prevent ringing. An anti-cogging term is added to make the motors “smoother”
> Cogging: Due to the location of the magnets and coils, there are places the rotor wants to sit with respect to the stator due to magnetic fields. We only want this on the hand side so the user feels a smoother response. If it was on the follower side, the compensation would read as the follower bumping into something. 
>
> Contact Spring:
> While T_env is above 80 mA, the leader and followers positions are recorded.

## Slide 12: Contact Term

- When the motor is held still, there is no acceleration, so estimated torque drops off
- Add a contact force term that adds a pushing force even when there is zero acceleration to take up that gap

> **Speaker notes:** Mathematically, both are one-way, saturated linear springs on the leader's travel past the contact point. The leader's also has a damper.
>
> Piecewise, for a push toward +counts (s = +1)
> Follower, with k = 0.5e-3 A/count, i_max = 0.05 A, T_r = 0.3 s:
> i_hold = { 0             x ≤ 0
>          { ρ · k · x     0 < x < 100 counts
>          { ρ · 0.05 A    x ≥ 100 counts
>
> Leader, with k_L = 2e-3 A/count, i_Lmax = 0.4 A, B_L = 1e-5 A/(count/s):
> i_L = { 0              − B_L·v_B     x ≤ 0
>       { −k_L · x       − B_L·v_B     0 < x < 200 counts
>       { −0.4 A         − B_L·v_B     x ≥ 200 counts
>
> For a push the other way (s = −1), both curves mirror: every sign flips, and the spring acts for x < 0.
> The three properties, in maths
> One-way (unilateral). The clamp at 0 means s·i_hold ≥ 0 and s·i_L,spring ≤ 0. The follower only pushes into the object, the leader only resists the hand, and neither ever pulls back toward the contact point.
>
> Saturated. |i_hold| ≤ 0.05 A and |i_L,spring| ≤ 0.4 A. Linear up to 100 and 200 counts respectively, then constant.
> Damping only on the leader, active throughout the latch, including when x ≤ 0.
> In physical units
> With K_t = 64.6 mN·m/A and 4000 counts per revolution, converting from A/count to N·m/rad:
> Stiffness
> Cap
> Follower spring0.5e-3 × 4000/2π × 0.0646 ≈ 0.021 N·m/rad (0.36 mN·m per degree)3.2 mN·m
> Leader spring≈ 0.082 N·m/rad (1.44 mN·m per degree)25.8 mN·m
> Leader damping1e-5 × 4000/2π × 0.0646 ≈ 4.1e-4 N·m·s/radnone
> It's the same as touching a spring-loaded stop with your hand. The stop can only be pushed, it pushes back proportionally to how far it's compressed, and its force tops out at the cap.
> Why it holds a press steady
> On each pass round the force loop, the follower's current changes by about:
> Δ|i_A| ≈ s · i_hold(x) − leak,      leak ≈ δ = 20 mA plus friction
> So the force grows while k·x > leak and decays while k·x < leak. It stays steady at x* = leak/k ≈ 20 mA ÷ 0.5e-3 A/count ≈ 40 counts.
> That makes the follower spring act like an integral controller on the leader's position. Its gain sets where the leader settles, about 40 counts past contact, but not how much force there is; your hand sets the force. The leader spring sets how stiff that contact point feels in your hand.

## Slide 16: Peer-to-Peer Coupling

No leader, no follower: both ESCs run the same law at 1 kHz. Each runs a disturbance observer for its own motor and for the other's (from its telemetry), which cancels friction, cogging and gravity locally and leaves each side's external force. A force channel keeps those forces equal and opposite; a position channel keeps the shafts aligned.

*Figure: Peer-to-Peer Coupling figure* — diagram_p2p_dob.png

> **Speaker notes:** Keio-style acceleration-based 4-channel bilateral control (Ohnishi, Katsura, Shimono). Firmware CHANGE 9 (P2P mode) and CHANGE 10 (in-session ripple/cogging calibration), 2026-11-18/19.
>
> This replaced PEER mode, a plain spring-damper between the shafts, which felt springy: a push had to stretch the spring before the other shaft moved, it saturated at 114 counts, and stiffer gains hit a 57 Hz buzz. Here a push drives the common (force) mode - both shafts accelerate together - and the observers' integral action drives the steady gap to zero.
>
> Kf sets the inertia the hand feels (apparent mass 2/Kf). Gains: wn 20 Hz, zeta 1, Kf 50 %, observer 80 rad/s. Details: Peer_to_Peer/README.md and references there.

## Slide 18: Two Approaches

|  | Leader–follower | Peer-to-peer |
|---|---|---|
| Roles | fixed: one leads, one follows | none — same law on both |
| Law runs on | the Pi, 500 Hz | both ESCs, 1 kHz |
| The hand feels | estimated contact force | the other side's force, via observers |
| Free motion | light — 0.4–0.6× B's rotor | 1.9× B's rotor (Kf 50 %) |
| Friction + gravity | cancelled by a fitted model | cancelled per session (calibration sweep) |
| Rigid vs soft | 20× at the follower, 1.2× at the hand | 3.2× at A, 3.1× at the hand |
| Hands off | stable | stable; ≈ 20 Hz wobble |

Two architectures, kept side by side. Leader–follower is lighter in free space because the follower is powered and never loads the hand; peer-to-peer gives honest physics — a stiffer, more readable contact with no designated master. Measured head to head on the next slides.

> **Speaker notes:** Leader-follower: the Pi runs the loop at 500 Hz; the follower tracks the leader, and the follower's estimated contact force is played back on the leader, with a contact spring on each side. Peer-to-peer: both ESCs at 1 kHz, disturbance observers, force and position channels (CHANGE 9/10).
>
> Rigid vs soft: leader-follower measures 20x apart at the follower but only 1.24x in leader travel (2026-10-29, before the leader spring). P2P: 3.2x at A and 3.1x in handle travel (2026-11-22). The soft object in the P2P run may have been the squishy foam rather than the sponge.
>
> Friction + gravity in P2P: a hands-off calibration sweep after every START fits each motor's ripple, cogging, Coulomb friction and (on arm A) gravity; the firmware cancels them.

|  | Teleop (leader–follower) | P2P (disturbance observers) |

P2P wins on contact — a stiffer handle, rigid vs soft you can feel, holds that don't fade without extra machinery. Teleop wins in free space and on taps. Both P2P losses trace to gains backed off for the ≈ 20 Hz wobble.
